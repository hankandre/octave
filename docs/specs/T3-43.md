## Goal

Left from any control in a screen's first column lands on the current screen's rail entry, on every screen with the rail, today's and later ones, without changing Right, select, Back or the rail's look.

## Cause (established from source)

The review called the cause "likely: the nearest-view search misses the edge guide". It is more precise than that, and partly wrong: the search does see the guide on Home, but scores it worse; on the placeholder screens it cannot see it at all.

1. **How Left is resolved.** No first-column control sets `nextFocusLeft` (T3-08's frozen tests forbid it in rows), so Android's `FocusFinder` picks by geometry from every focusable rect (`FocusFinder.java`, AOSP `platform_frameworks_base` main, `isBetterCandidate`, `beamBeats`, `getWeightedDistanceFor`, `isCandidate`, `minorAxisDistance`):
   - a rect is a Left candidate only if `src.left > dest.left` and it lies at least partly left of the source;
   - a candidate vertically overlapping the source (in its beam) beats one that does not;
   - between two in-beam candidates the smaller `13·major² + minor²` wins, where `minor` is the distance between the **vertical centres**.
2. **What the guide offers the search.** A `TVFocusGuideView` with destinations adds only itself to the focusables while focus is outside it, and its `requestFocus` sends focus to the destination (react-native-tvos 0.88.1, `ReactViewGroup.kt:1362-1383` `addFocusables`, `:1442-1484` `requestFocus`, `:1311-1319` `isTVFocusGuide` = destinations set or `autoFocus`). `shell-edge` is one full-height rect, 4 canvas px (2 dp) wide, at the content's left edge (`app-shell.tsx:205`, `:229`); its vertical centre is mid-screen.
3. **Home** (first card of a row, `rowInsetStart` 24 canvas px right of the content edge, `row-geometry.ts`): the guide is a candidate and in the beam, but a rail entry level with the card is in the beam too. The entry is about 10 dp further away on the major axis and about 0 dp on the minor axis; the guide is closer on the major axis but its centre is up to several hundred dp from the card's. Example: a card on a row whose centre is 160 dp below mid-screen scores the guide 13·10² + 160² ≈ 26,900 and the Settings entry 13·20² + ~0² ≈ 5,200. Settings wins, as the review saw from Recently added's first card. The coordinates are reasoned from the layout code, not measured.
4. **Search, Settings, Accounts placeholders** (`FirstScreen.tsx:72`, `besideRail: { paddingLeft: 0 }`): the first tile's left edge equals the guide's left edge, so `src.left > dest.left` fails and the guide is never a candidate. No entry is in the tile's beam, so the entry with the nearest centre wins: Home (the rail holds Search, Home, Settings and the account today; `signed-in-app.tsx:80` passes `libraries={[]}`), as the review saw.
5. `enableCustomFocusSearchOnClippedElementsAndroid` defaults to `false` (`ReactNativeFeatureFlagsDefaults.kt:58`), so the scroll views add no search of their own; the app's flag overrides were not checked (**unverified**, milestone 2 shows it).

So one thin guide competing with the entries on geometry cannot win from every place. The fix must take the individual entries out of the search while focus is in the content.

## What Jest can and cannot prove

Jest has no focus engine and no `FocusFinder`: no test can move focus, and `TVFocusGuideView`'s native `setDestinations` runs only when `Platform.isTV`. A Jest test proves the precondition: which guide is pointed at which view, and that it follows the current entry across navigation (the same reading T3-08 and T3-09 took). It cannot prove that Android chooses the guide, which is why the cause above is argued from source and items 1 and 4 need the emulator. A Robolectric test of `ReactViewGroup` was weighed and not chosen: it would test a copy of the layout, not the app's.

## Approach

- Point `rail-guide`, not each control: one change in the shell covers every screen, including screens later tasks build, and needs no screen to know a native tag.
- Use `destinations` through `pointGuide`, not `autoFocus`: `autoFocus` returns to the entry focused last, which after browsing the open rail and pressing Right is not the current one.
- Not chosen:
  - `nextFocusLeft` on every first-column control. It needs every screen to plumb the entry's tag, and T3-08's frozen `media-row.test.tsx:76` and `row-list.test.tsx` forbid `nextFocusLeft` in rows.
  - Making the other entries unfocusable while the rail is closed. The rail opens by a React state change after the focus event, so a quick Up or Down after Left would find nothing.
  - A wider or per-row edge guide. It still competes on geometry, and on the placeholders it shares the tile's left edge.

## Risks and out of scope

- **Side effect, intended:** with the rail a single rect while focus is in the content, no entry is an Up or Down candidate from the content (a full-height rect is never one). Today Up from the hero's top or Down from the last row may fall into the rail by geometry. After this change, those presses do nothing, which fits "Nothing moves under you" and T5-09's call 3.
- **Default focus.** If Android hunts for a default focus (nothing preferred), it now lands on the current entry instead of Search, since the rail comes first in the tree. Milestone 2(f) checks the opening focus is unchanged.
- **TalkBack.** A pointed guide gets the library's focus-guide accessibility delegate (`ReactViewManager.kt:609-637`, `ReactViewGroup.kt:1157-1232`), which sends accessibility focus on the rail itself to the current entry. `shell-edge` already has it; whether swiping within the rail still reaches every entry is **unverified**. It is listed as untested in the PR, for T3-10 or a TalkBack pass.
- **Pre-existing, not changed:** a Right pressed in the ~160 ms before the open rail widens could hit `shell-edge` and bounce back to the entry. It needs `returnTag` to be unset, which happens only before any content focus.
- Out of scope: Up into the hero (T5-09), the row end fade (T5-09), fast-press focus loss (F6, deferred), and retiring `shell-edge`.

## Spec check

Checked 2026-11-26 in a fresh context against `pnpm task show T3-43` (4 acceptance items; the table has 4), `AGENTS.md`, `DESIGN.md` §§7.1 and 9, `docs/specs/decisions-product-owner-2026-11-26d.md`, the code on `d9e7a2fa`, and react-native-tvos 0.88.1 as installed (`ReactViewGroup.kt`, `ReactViewManager.kt`, `Libraries/Components/TV/TVFocusGuideView.js`).

**Cause confirmed from source.** `addFocusables` adds only the guide while no descendant has focus (`ReactViewGroup.kt:1362-1383`); `requestFocus` sends focus to the first destination found and returns `false`, without searching, when a destination is set but not in the tree (`:1442-1460`); `isTVFocusGuide` is destinations set or `autoFocus`, not the traps (`:1311-1319`); the flag default is `false` (`ReactNativeFeatureFlagsDefaults.kt:58`) and no app code overrides it (grep). `TVFocusGuideView.js` clears destinations on render only when `focusable === false`, which `rail-guide` never sets, so an imperative `setDestinations` survives re-renders; unresolvable views are filtered, so a missing view yields `[]`.

**Attack on the design (no trap found).**
- *Inside the rail.* Once an entry holds focus, `focusedChildOfFocusGuide` is non-null (the direct child, the group or the ScrollView, whose nearest guide is `rail-guide`), so the guide lists its children again. `trapFocusUp/Down/Left` call `FocusFinder.findNextFocus(this, …)`, which calls this same `addFocusables`: Up, Down and Left behave as today. The guide itself is never a candidate from inside (its rect contains every entry).
- *Right.* Not trapped, so the search reaches the root, where `findNextUserSpecifiedFocus` reads `nextFocusRight` before geometry. Unchanged. With `returnTag` unset the geometric fallback is the pre-existing case the spec names.
- *Back and select.* `focusView` on the entry or the place; no guide involved. Unchanged.
- *Missing entry.* No registered view gives `pointGuide(guide, [])`, so the rail stops being a guide and its entries are candidates as today: degraded to today's behaviour, never stuck. A stale tag (entry unmounted since pointing) makes `requestFocus` return `false`: one Left does nothing, focus stays where it was, Back still opens the rail. Not a trap.
- *Current entry scrolled out of the list.* `requestFocus` on the entry needs only visibility, not on-screen; the list has `scrollsChildToFocus: false`, and the entry's `onFocus` runs `useRailReveal`, exactly the path Back takes today. No new risk.
- *Route changes.* Root changes move `current` and re-run the effect; a pushed screen keeps `current`; `play` unmounts the rail and its remount re-points on mount. Refs attach in the commit before passive effects, so the view is registered when the effect runs.
- *Focus recovery.* `rail-guide` becoming a guide also makes it the `findParentFocusGuide` of a removed focused entry (`recoverFocus`, `:653-739`): focus then goes to the rail's first focusable (Search) instead of Android's default. Stays in the rail; acceptable.
- *TalkBack.* The delegate is installed only with TalkBack on (`ReactViewManager.kt:613-637`). It makes the guide focusable and redirects accessibility focus on it to the current entry; the children stay separate nodes, so every entry stays reachable, but backward linear traversal can bounce from the guide to the current entry. Not a D-pad trap; unverified on a device.

**Verdict: Approved.**
